Research 01

I’m a public health researcher, with a background in mathematics and machine learning. I enjoy working with data, making sense of its structure, asking what it can tell us, and exploring it.

Public health, people, and environments

Why certain social groups experience higher burdens of illness and how social advantages or disadvantages translate into health? This is the question we are interested in when working in social and environmental epidemiology. We can explore this narrower and connect if to one of the most important sides of health: mental health. Mental health problems can be associated with, or can even be the cause of, many physical health problems. Questions that help us to understand the effects of socioeconomic factors on population mental health can guide us to set better public health policies.

Cognitive Modelling and computational neuroscience

How the human brain process information, learn from them, decide how and when to use them, and adapt its behaviour, patterns, and even beliefs in different situations of life. We study this question by combining mathematics, artificial intelligence, psychology, and neurobiology. Understanding how the human brain learns and adapts, can help us not only to understand cognitive development and develop methods for treating different psychological problems, but also to improve artificial intelligence.

Meta-Learning

How to learn how to learn?! Learning to learn comes into play when you want your model to adapt very fast to new tasks. You do not train it to do a specific task; you train it to learn from learning to do different tasks.

Causal Inference

It is not always causality! An association can reveal a pattern without explaining what would happen if we changed something. Causal inference addresses that distinction, using explicit assumptions and careful study design to investigate the effects of exposures and interventions. In health research, this means thinking closely about confounding, the timing of measurements, and whether the available data can support the question being asked.

AI in Health

Artificial intelligence can help identify patterns in complex health data, but a useful model needs to do more than perform well on a single dataset, especially when coming to health. Its value also depends on how it handles its own uncertainty, how reliably it works across populations, and whether its outputs help answer a meaningful clinical or public health question. Careful validation, attention to bias, and a clear account of a model’s limitations are central to that work. Artificial intelligence can absolutely do very more than it is doing now, and we should see this potencial to use it correctly in health.

Evidence, methods, and reproducibility

After all above, we should be able to develop reproducible workflows based on the evidence we have and with the methods we use. If they are not enough, we can develop evidence and methods instead of developing new workflows everytime.
